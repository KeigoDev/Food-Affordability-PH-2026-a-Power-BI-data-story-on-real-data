# Food Affordability PH 2026 — a Power BI data story on real data

All figures come from official or published sources: **PSA** (Philippine Statistics Authority), the **ECB** (exchange rates), **SWS** (hunger survey), **DOLE wage boards** and the **Department of Agriculture**. Latest data month: **September 2026**. Latest GDP quarter: **Q2 2026**.

## 1. The problem
In 2026, the Philippine economy is slowing while prices climb again, and rice, the biggest item in a family's budget, is driving it.

| Signal | Latest | A year earlier | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline inflation | **7.2%** (Sep 2026) | 1.7% (Sep 2025) | PSA CPI |
| Rice inflation | **+20.3%** | −16.9% | PSA CPI |
| Rice retail price, regular-milled | **₱50.10/kg** | ₱40.23/kg (+24.5%) | PSA |
| US dollar | **₱62.62** Sep average (Oct 2026 average so far: ₱63.02; latest daily rate ₱63.94 on 26 Oct, a record low for the peso) | ₱57.29 (peso 9.3% weaker) | ECB, BAP |
| BSP outlook (22 Oct 2026) | policy rate raised 0.5 pt to **5.5%**; inflation forecast **6.1%** for 2026, **6.0%** for 2027 | — | [BSP via Manila Times](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/10/27/business/top-business/inflation-threatens-prolonged-tightening/2454325) |
| GDP growth | **2.3%** (Q2 2026) | 5.4% (Q2 2025) | PSA |
| Purchasing power of ₱1 (2018 = 1.00) | **₱0.73** | ₱0.78 | PSA |
| Families experiencing hunger | **22.6%** (Sep 2026) | 22.0% (Sep 2025) | SWS |
| Rice a NCR minimum daily wage buys | **14.9 kg** | 17.6 kg at the Oct 2025 peak | PSA + DOLE |

**What happened to rice:** after the import tariff was cut to 15% (Jul 2024), cheap imports pushed retail rice down from ₱51 to ₱40/kg by Oct 2025. Imports were then suspended (Sep 2025), and a new price-linked tariff started in Jan 2026. Retail rice jumped back to ₱51.51 by April 2026. Rice inflation went from −17% to +20% in a year, while the peso weakened and GDP growth slowed.

**The question the report answers:** *Which regions are hit hardest by the 2026 rice price shock, and where should relief (such as subsidized rice) go first?* 12 of 18 regions have rice inflation above the national 20.3%. The highest are Western Visayas (30.0%), Bicol (29.3%) and Cagayan Valley (28.6%).

## Interactive web dashboard
**Every KPI follows the filters.** The cards and story numbers show the **To** month of the date slicer compared with 12 months earlier, and lows and peaks are measured within the selected range. On page 4, the island-group slicer switches the cards to that group's average and recounts the regions above the national rate (regional data is the latest-month snapshot, so the date slicer doesn't apply there). Page 1 also shows a **dollar forecast to December 2026** (now ₱64.76, range ₱63.68–65.85; November ₱64.35): the straight-line trend of the last 12 monthly USD/PHP averages (about +₱0.41 a month), started from the latest ECB daily rate (₱63.94 on 26 Oct) so it doesn't lag a fast-moving peso, with a 95% range. The newest month can be **provisional**: until the ECB publishes a month's average, `extract_data.py` averages its daily rates so far (October 2026 to the 26th) and marks the row `PROVISIONAL`; it is saved to `data/fx_monthly.csv`, shown on the chart as the first dashed step, and used in the forecast fit. It's a simple projection, not an official forecast, and it shows only when the date range ends at the latest month.

**Double-click `dashboard.html`** to open the interactive dashboard in any browser (it needs internet once to load the d3 chart library). It's a single file with the real data built in, and it follows your light/dark system theme. The header has two download buttons: **Download Power BI project (.pbip)** gives a zip with the Power BI project, raw data, theme, logo, DAX, Power Query and this guide (also saved as `downloads/FoodAffordabilityPH_PowerBI.zip`), and **Download raw data (Excel)** gives the extracted workbook. Both files are built into `dashboard.html`, so the buttons work even when the HTML is shared on its own. After refreshing the data, rebuild it with `python3 build_web_dashboard.py`. Its page source is `web-dashboard/index.html`. It has the same 4 pages (as tabs), a date-range slicer (From/To, "Last 12 months", "2026 only"), an island-group slicer, cross-filtering (click a region and the cards switch to it), sortable tables, hover tooltips, and click-to-compare on the hunger chart. Screenshots: `preview/web-*.jpg`.

### Stage 1 — Extract (Python → Excel)
```
pip install pandas openpyxl
python3 extract_data.py
```
It saves `raw/ph_food_economy_raw.xlsx`, one sheet per source, **left uncleaned on purpose**: wide month columns, `.`/`..` placeholders for unreleased months, and region names that differ between PSA tables.

| Sheet | Source | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `psa_rice_prices` | [PSA OpenSTAT 0042M4ARN01](https://openstat.psa.gov.ph/PXWeb/pxweb/en/DB/DB__2M__2018NEW/) | Retail price of regular-milled rice, PH + 18 regions, monthly 2024–2026 |
| `psa_inflation` | [PSA OpenSTAT 0012M4ACP23](https://openstat.psa.gov.ph/PXWeb/pxweb/en/DB/DB__2M__PI__CPI__2018NEW/) | CPI year-on-year %: all items, food, rice; PH + regions |
| `psa_peso_power` | PSA OpenSTAT 0012M4ACP24 | Purchasing power of the peso (2018 = 1.00) |
| `psa_gdp_growth` | [PSA OpenSTAT 0022B5BEXQ2](https://openstat.psa.gov.ph/PXWeb/pxweb/en/DB/DB__2B__NA__QT__1SUM/) | Real GDP growth by quarter (constant 2018 prices) |
| `ecb_fx` | [ECB Data Portal, EXR](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/EXR) | Monthly PHP/EUR and USD/EUR; USD/PHP = PHP÷USD |
| `sws_hunger` | SWS releases via news reports (links in the sheet) | Involuntary hunger, % of families, per survey |
| `ncr_min_wage` | DOLE / RTWPB-NCR wage orders (links in the sheet) | NCR daily minimum wage |
| `rice_policy_events` | DA, USDA FAS (links in the sheet) | Tariff cut, import suspension, new tariff |
| `region_lookup` | Built for this project | Matches PSA's two region-naming styles |

SWS, the wage orders and the policy events have no API, so their values are typed into the script with a source link per row.

### Stage 2 — Clean & transform (Power Query)
In Power BI Desktop: **Transform data → Manage Parameters → New** `RawFile` (Text) = full path to the raw Excel file. Then for each block in `power_query.m`: **New Source → Blank Query → Advanced Editor → paste → rename**. Do them in the file's order.

Cleaning steps used:
- **Promote headers** and **unpivot** wide month columns into rows (`Table.UnpivotOtherColumns`)
- **Remove totals** ("Annual", "Ave" columns)
- **Handle missing values**: `fnToNumber` turns `.` and `..` (not yet released) into null, then those rows are removed
- **Parse dates** from text ("2025 January" → 2025-01-01; "2025-2026 Q2" → 2026-04-01)
- **Fix types** (Date, Decimal, Text) with the `en-US` culture, so decimals load correctly on any PC
- **Trim** PSA's indent dots ("..NCR") and **merge** with `Region` (inner join, which also drops "Areas Outside NCR")
- **Pivot** ECB currencies and **derive** USD/PHP = PHP per EUR ÷ USD per EUR
- **Map** CPI codes to readable items (All items / Food / Rice)

Then **Close & Apply**. To refresh with new months later, run `extract_data.py` again and click **Refresh** in Power BI.

### Stage 3 — Model (star schema)
1. **Modeling → New table** → paste the `Calendar` formula from the top of `measures.dax`. **Mark as date table** on `Date`, and sort `Month` by `Month Number`.
2. **Home → Enter data** → an empty table named `_Measures`.
3. **Model view** → create these relationships (one-to-many, single direction):

| From (one) | To (many) |
|---|---|
| `Region[region_key]` | `RicePrice[region_key]`, `Inflation[region_key]` |
| `Calendar[Date]` | `RicePrice[month]`, `Inflation[month]`, `FX[month]`, `PesoPower[month]`, `Hunger[month]`, `GDP[quarter_start]` |

`Wage` and `Events` stay unconnected; measures read them directly.
4. Hide the helper columns `rice_label` and `cpi_label` (right-click → Hide in report view).

### Stage 4 — Calculate (DAX)
Add every measure from `measures.dax` to `_Measures` (Modeling → New measure). With no region selected, measures show the national figure. Select a region (slicer or click) and they switch to that region.

Formatting:
- **Decimal, 1 place:** inflation, GDP, hunger, kg, pts measures (they're already in %, so don't use the % format)
- **Percentage, 1 place:** `Rice Price Change YoY %`, `Peso Weakening YoY %`
- **Decimal, 2 places:** `Rice Price…`, `USD PHP…`, `Peso Purchasing Power…`

## 4. Caveats
- PSA revises recent months, so re-run stage 1 before presenting. GDP for Q3 2026 wasn't released at extraction time.
- SWS releases are taken from news reports of the official SWS results; check them against [sws.org.ph](https://www.sws.org.ph) if you cite them formally.
- The wage is NCR's; other regions have their own wage boards. That's why "kg of rice per wage" uses NCR rice prices.
- The NCR wage order NCR-28 (₱755) took effect 26 Sep 2026, so it first shows in October data.

## 5. Interview / portfolio talking points
- **Problem:** a rice price shock on top of a weaker peso and slower growth. Inflation is back above target and hunger is stuck above 20%.
- **Approach:** an automated Python extract from official APIs, documented Power Query cleaning, a star schema, DAX with time intelligence (latest vs year-ago), and a cross-checked validation step.
- **Insight:** policy moves on rice imports swung the retail price by about ₱11/kg in both directions within 18 months. The 2026 shock is hitting Visayas and parts of Luzon and Mindanao hardest.
- **Action:** target relief to regions where rice inflation is above the national rate (12 of 18).
